# How to unsubscribe from postal mail

> ↩️ A previous version of this article appeared on [MinimalistHero](https://medium.com/the-minimalist/7-ways-to-unsubscribe-from-physical-mail-d1b91673b1f4).

Sometimes external forces make [living minimally](https://medium.com/the-minimalist/six-steps-towards-minimalist-living-8a1c4ff1b8e3) a challenge. One such force is the postal service. Every day a government employee drops off a new batch of clutter — and ironically most of this clutter is about convincing us to buy more clutter.

[I'd like to cancel my mail](https://www.youtube.com/embed/Hox-ni8geIw?feature=oembed)

*Kramer trying to cancel his mail on Seinfeld*

While cancelling your mail might be infeasible—if not impossible—there are a few steps you can take to significantly decrease the amount of junk mail you receive. None of these steps are immediate, and most require a bit of patience.

### 1. Opt-out of catalogs

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1600/1*K5ROugLRDYPpTpg6aOQq9A.png)

*The Data & Marketing Association* is a trade organization that shares your data and mailing address with its member corporations. You can [create an account on their website](http://dmachoice.thedma.org) and pay $4 to opt-out of their lists.

### 2. Opt-out of credit and insurance offers

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1600/1*l2tZSc9d16t0hPxdlDKKhw.png)

You can reduce your credit and insurance offers from Equifax, Experian, Innovis, and TransUnion at [OptOutPreScreen.com](https://www.optoutprescreen.com/selection). They offer a 5-year opt-out online. Absurdly, you must print and mail a form to opt-out permanently.

### 3. Join the “National Do Not Mail List”

While not as official as it sounds, the [National Do Not Mail List](http://www.directmail.com/mail_preference/) will remove you from DirectMail.com’s client corporations. The signup form is simple enough, as long as you feel comfortable giving your email address to a company whose primary business is spam.

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1600/1*jwyVh1ftA1f5iFzFxpcjxA.png)

*Some of DirectMail.com’s clients*

### 4. Say goodbye to the Yellow Pages

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1600/1*NzXm2_k2kif-cFKpE1az7w.png)

*Photo via [treehugger](https://www.treehugger.com/sustainable-product-design/dear-bell-yellow-pages-we-have-a-thing-called-the-internet.html)*

This one may come as a surprise: you can actually say no to the Yellow Pages. [YellowPagesOptOut.com](https://www.yellowpagesoptout.com) will help you find your local distributor and provide instructions to opt-out. Easy!

### 5. Remove yourself from RetailMeNot & Share Local Media

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1600/1*4VUAgVcdkztIWC4y8wVU3Q.png)

Next time you get a book of coupons in the mail, take a look at the sender. It’s most likely Save.com (formerly RetailMeNot, which was formerly RedPlum). The good news: they make it painless to [remove yourself from Save.com's list](https://save.com/mailing/delivery-options) online. The bad news: if your mail carrier is as careless as mine, you’ll still receive the coupons—just with someone else’s name and address.

You can also [opt-out of Share Local Media](https://opt-out.sharelocalmedia.com/), which is used by Casper, Harry's, Grubhub, and Lyft among others.

### 6. See if this non-profit can help

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1600/1*aUXuVyflCCkGTRQ6BfDMVw.png)

*CatalogChoice lets you keep track of your unsubscribes*

[CatalogChoice](http://www.catalogchoice.org) is a Berkeley-based nonprofit operated by [The Story of Stuff](https://www.storyofstuff.org/), with a goal of ending junk mail. While their database is incomplete, they may be able to help with some of the larger offenders. At very least, it’s a noble cause worth supporting 🤗
